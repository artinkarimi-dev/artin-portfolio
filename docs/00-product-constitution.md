# Artin Portfolio — Product Constitution

## Status

Foundation document.

This document defines the high-level purpose and non-negotiable principles of Artin Karimi's personal website.

It does not define the final sitemap, final visual design, final technology stack, or final copy.

Those decisions will be documented separately as the project progresses.

---

## 1. What we are building

We are building a personal website for Artin Karimi, a frontend developer who works primarily on real web projects and modern frontend experiences.

The website is not simply a place to list skills and projects.

It is both:

1. a presentation of Artin's work
2. an example of Artin's work

The quality of the website itself must provide evidence of Artin's frontend judgment, visual attention, interaction quality, implementation discipline, and care for the finished product.

---

## 2. Primary business objective

The primary objective is to create enough interest, clarity, and trust that a relevant visitor wants to start a professional conversation with Artin.

The most important visitor is a potential employer or client.

This may include:

* a company considering hiring Artin
* a freelance client considering giving Artin a project
* a hiring manager
* a recruiter with a relevant opportunity

Secondary value should exist for developers and potential collaborators.

---

## 3. The visitor's ideal mental journey

The intended experience can be summarized as:

### First impression

"This looks unusually good."

The visual quality should earn attention.

### Shortly afterward

"This is clear. I understand what he does."

Visual ambition must not obscure comprehension.

### After exploring the work

"He has actually built real things."

The website must show evidence, not only claims.

### After deeper exploration

"He thinks carefully about products, problems, details, and implementation."

The visitor should discover judgment behind the output.

### Final reaction

"I would like to talk to him."

The site should naturally lead toward contact, collaboration, hiring, or continued professional connection.

The strategic progression is:

**Impressive → Clear → Credible → Hireable**

---

## 4. North Star

The website should be:

**Beautiful enough to stop people.
Simple enough to understand.
Real enough to trust.
Memorable enough to contact.**

A longer strategic description:

A beautifully crafted, unusually polished personal website that first makes people curious, then makes Artin's work obvious, and finally gives them a reason to trust him with theirs.

---

## 5. What "special" means

"Special" does not mean unusual for the sake of being unusual.

It does not mean adding features people will not use.

It does not mean maximizing animation, 3D, visual noise, or technical complexity.

For this project, special means:

* unusually high attention to detail
* a strong and recognizable visual personality
* excellent presentation of real work
* thoughtful interactions
* clear information hierarchy
* memorable moments with purpose
* coherent behavior across the entire product
* small details that reward attention
* a website that feels deliberately designed rather than assembled from common portfolio patterns

Every unusual idea must still earn its place.

---

## 6. Visual personality

Current direction:

**Dark-first, premium, editorial, product-oriented and developer-crafted.**

The website should feel modern and highly designed without appearing loud, childish, gimmicky, or stereotypically "tech."

Desired visual qualities include:

* confident dark surfaces
* excellent typography
* precise spacing
* strong composition
* controlled contrast
* strong visual hierarchy
* generous negative space
* sophisticated project imagery
* subtle depth
* carefully timed motion
* polished micro-interactions
* restrained decorative elements

The exact color palette has not yet been selected.

Dark-first should not become pure black everywhere by default.

Near-black, charcoal, layered dark surfaces, warm or neutral text tones, and a carefully selected accent may later be explored.

The final accent color must be intentional rather than selected because it is fashionable.

---

## 7. Visual anti-patterns

The following should not be used by default:

* neon developer aesthetic
* Matrix green
* hacker/cyberpunk visual language
* fake terminal interfaces
* code rain
* fake command prompts
* random source code used as decoration
* excessive glow
* excessive gradients
* glassmorphism on every surface
* generic SaaS feature grids
* identical rounded cards everywhere
* meaningless 3D models
* huge animated background effects
* scroll hijacking
* custom cursor tricks that hurt usability
* excessive parallax
* excessive blur
* excessive motion
* an animation simply because the library makes it easy
* percentage skill bars
* giant walls of technology logos

None of these are absolutely forbidden if a future concept provides a very strong functional reason, but they require explicit approval.

---

## 8. Audience clarity

Many primary visitors will not be developers.

The website must never require technical knowledge to understand Artin's value.

For example, instead of expecting a client to understand:

"Built a React frontend integrated with REST endpoints"

the top-level explanation should answer questions such as:

* What was being built?
* What problem existed?
* What did Artin change?
* What became better?
* What was actually delivered?

Technical detail should remain available for visitors who care about it.

The site should therefore operate on two levels:

### Level 1 — Human / business understanding

Clear, visual, outcome-oriented.

### Level 2 — Technical depth

Architecture, implementation, stack, engineering decisions and trade-offs.

Technical depth should enrich the experience, not dominate it.

---

## 9. Why Artin

One of the most important strategic requirements is a credible answer to:

**Why should someone choose Artin rather than another frontend developer?**

The answer must not be built from claims that virtually every developer can make.

Weak examples include:

* passionate developer
* clean code
* creative
* hardworking
* problem solver
* fast learner
* pixel perfect
* good communication

The website should instead demonstrate differentiators through real evidence.

Three current proof themes:

### A. Beyond the happy path

A real frontend product is not only the successful screenshot.

There are:

* loading states
* failures
* empty states
* integrations
* deployment differences
* production bugs
* API inconsistencies
* responsiveness
* unexpected edge cases

Artin's work should demonstrate awareness of the full product experience.

### B. It should feel finished

"Technically works" is not the same as "feels good to use."

Frontend quality also includes:

* visual polish
* interaction
* responsiveness
* perceived quality
* consistency
* error behavior
* loading behavior
* accessibility
* details

The personal website itself must become evidence for this principle.

### C. From problem to shipped result

The value is not simply knowing a technology.

The stronger story is the ability to take:

**an idea / requirement / problem → decisions → implementation → debugging → a usable result**

Real shipped work is stronger proof than self-reported skill.

These themes must later be validated against Artin's strongest projects and experiences before becoming final public claims.

---

## 10. Work over skill lists

The website should emphasize evidence of capability rather than a long inventory of technologies.

Technologies can appear where contextually useful, but should not become the main story.

The visitor should remember:

* what Artin made
* what problems he solved
* how polished the result felt
* how he thought about the work

not whether a React icon appeared in a grid.

---

## 11. Project storytelling

Strong projects should be treated more like concise product stories than generic portfolio cards.

At the simplest level, a project should communicate:

**What was needed?
What did Artin do?
What was difficult or important?
What was shipped?**

A deeper layer may cover:

* technical architecture
* stack
* constraints
* decisions
* trade-offs
* failures and fixes
* production lessons
* source code
* live experience

The amount of detail should depend on the importance of the project.

Not every piece of work needs a massive case study.

Small experiments, components, discoveries, fixes, and frontend explorations may later have a lighter presentation format.

This allows the site to feel alive without requiring Artin to constantly build complete new projects.

---

## 12. Personal brand

The website should communicate that Artin is a real developer who:

* builds
* learns
* encounters problems
* makes decisions
* improves things
* ships
* shares what he discovers

The desired brand is not:

* guru
* motivational influencer
* corporate consultant
* tutorial machine
* comedy-only developer
* overly formal engineer persona

It should feel young, real, capable, curious and professional.

Professional does not mean stiff.

Personality should exist without reducing credibility.

---

## 13. Personal image

Artin wants a visible human presence, but only as a supporting element.

Current direction:

* a small personal photograph is appropriate
* it should increase trust and identity
* it should not dominate the hero
* the website should still primarily be remembered for the work, design and thinking

The final location and treatment of the photograph will be decided during visual design.

---

## 14. Language and tone

Primary language:

**English**

The English should sound like a good human conversation.

Desired tone:

* simple
* natural
* confident
* direct
* warm
* modern
* professional
* occasionally playful when appropriate

Avoid:

* academic wording
* unnecessarily advanced vocabulary
* corporate jargon
* startup cliché language
* exaggerated self-praise
* AI-sounding marketing prose

The copy should be understandable to someone whose first language is not English.

---

## 15. Persian experience

Persian must be available through a deliberate language switch.

English remains the default.

Persian is not merely translated text.

The Persian version must behave correctly as an RTL interface.

Requirements include:

* correct document language
* correct directionality
* RTL-aware layout
* intentional typography
* mirrored directional behavior where appropriate
* correct icon direction where meaning changes with direction
* preserved visual hierarchy

Language selection should not use national flags.

---

## 16. Contact and professional ecosystem

The personal website is a central hub, not an isolated page.

Relevant external destinations include:

### Telegram

A direct and low-friction path for collaboration and communication.

### LinkedIn

Professional identity, networking and hiring context.

### GitHub

Technical proof and source code.

### Instagram

Artin's public building journey, frontend content and personal brand.

These channels should eventually be presented with context and purpose instead of existing only as four anonymous social icons in a footer.

---

## 17. Motion philosophy

Motion should communicate quality.

It may help with:

* hierarchy
* continuity
* state change
* feedback
* spatial understanding
* revealing detail
* creating memorable transitions

Motion should not:

* delay access to content
* interfere with scrolling
* make reading difficult
* exist everywhere
* make the site feel like a demo reel
* significantly damage performance

The best motion may be subtle enough that users feel the quality without consciously analyzing every animation.

Reduced-motion preferences must be respected.

---

## 18. Performance philosophy

A frontend developer's own website should not be unnecessarily slow.

Performance is part of the demonstration of skill.

The project should aim for strong Core Web Vitals and a fast perceived experience.

Current targets:

* LCP: 2.5 seconds or better
* INP: 200 milliseconds or better
* CLS: 0.1 or better

Large media, fonts, animation libraries, JavaScript, and third-party integrations must justify their cost.

Visual ambition must be engineered intelligently.

---

## 19. Accessibility philosophy

Accessibility is part of quality, not a final checklist.

The project should target WCAG 2.2 AA where reasonably applicable.

Accessibility considerations should include:

* semantic structure
* keyboard navigation
* visible focus
* contrast
* target sizing
* motion preferences
* readable text
* correct labels
* logical document structure
* accessible interactive states

A beautiful interaction that some users cannot operate is not considered polished.

---

## 20. Responsive philosophy

Mobile is not a compressed desktop screenshot.

The website must be deliberately composed for multiple viewport sizes.

Responsive design should preserve:

* hierarchy
* storytelling
* readability
* interaction quality
* project presentation
* navigation clarity
* CTA visibility

Some compositions may need to change rather than merely shrink.

---

## 21. Technical philosophy

The technology stack has intentionally not been selected yet.

Technology must follow the needs of the product.

Do not begin with:

"Artin uses React, therefore the website must use a particular React setup."

Instead determine:

* routing requirements
* rendering requirements
* SEO needs
* localization architecture
* content update needs
* animation needs
* deployment model
* performance constraints
* maintainability requirements

Then select the simplest strong architecture that fits.

Artin's frontend experience with React is relevant, but it does not override architectural reasoning.

---

No final sitemap has been approved.

Potential concepts discussed so far include:

## 24. Things the site should not become

This website should not become:

* a résumé with animations
* a copied Awwwards concept
* a clone of another creator
* a generic React portfolio template
* an online CV
* a collection of technology logos
* a giant biography
* an effects playground
* a visual experience that hides the work
* an inaccessible interactive experiment
* a site whose complexity exists only to impress developers
* a page designed exclusively for recruiters
* a page designed exclusively for freelance clients

It should remain a coherent representation of Artin as both a professional and a builder.

---

## 25. Decision hierarchy

When two ideas conflict, prefer them in this approximate order:

1. clarity
2. credibility
3. usability
4. quality of work presentation
5. accessibility
6. performance
7. maintainability
8. visual memorability
9. novelty

This does not mean visual design is unimportant.

The website is expected to have exceptionally strong visual quality.

It means visual novelty cannot destroy the product underneath it.

---

## 26. Definition of success

The project is successful if a relevant visitor can:

1. quickly understand what Artin does
2. become interested because the website itself feels highly crafted
3. understand his work without technical expertise
4. find convincing evidence of real ability
5. discover deeper technical credibility if desired
6. understand something meaningful about how Artin works
7. remember him after leaving the website
8. easily find a suitable way to contact or follow him

The strongest result is not simply a compliment about the site's design.

The strongest result is a professional conversation that begins because the site created enough interest and trust.

---

## 27. Final governing principle

Do not ask:

**"How can we make this portfolio look more impressive?"**

Ask:

**"How can every part of this experience make Artin easier to understand, easier to trust, and harder to forget?"**

That question should govern the project.
